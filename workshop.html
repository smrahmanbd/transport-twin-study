Digital Twins for Transportation Workshop

From “what is a twin?” on the first morning to a working, camera-fed signal twin in SUMO and a deployment plan for your own city by the third afternoon. Engineers code in the labs; managers and planners follow a parallel non-coding track that designs the stack, the business case and the rollout.

Outcomes

By the end, participants can

Day 1 · Understand

  • Tell a model, a shadow and a twin apart, and expose “twin-washing”.
  • Map a transport system to the five layers.
  • Audit the data and standards a twin needs.
  • Build and run a SUMO network.

Day 2 · Build

  • Estimate capacity and calibrate with GEH.
  • Connect a twin controller to SUMO with TraCI.
  • Replace perfect sensors with a virtual camera and measure what that costs.
  • Choose software for each layer.

Day 3 · Apply

  • Compare controllers fairly across random seeds.
  • Frame a research question or pilot around a real gap.
  • Present a working prototype or plan.
  • Write a 10-step plan from SUMO to a real camera twin.

Capstone

The team challenge

Teams of 3–5 pick one brief on day 1 afternoon, work on it in the labs, and present on day 3 (10 minutes plus 5 for questions). Each team should mix both tracks.

BriefTrackWhat to deliver
A. Beat actuated control at medium demand across five seedsEngineersChanged twin_controller.py, results for seeds 1–5 with ranges, and why it works
B. Fuse camera and loops to close the sensing gapEngineersFusion logic, detection and time-loss comparison with the camera-only twin
C. Add a pedestrian crossing at J2 with a safe walk timeEngineersNetwork change, controller rule, effect on vehicles and pedestrians
D. Pilot plan for a real corridor in your cityManagersCharter, software stack from the Software guide, 10-step rollout, risks, budget headings
E. Research proposal on one of the ten gapsMixedQuestion, design, measures, threats to validity, data plan

Before the workshop

Pre-workshop setup and pre-work

Participants (send one week before)

  • Laptop with Windows, macOS or Linux, 8 GB RAM or more, admin rights
  • Python 3.9 or newer
  • Run: pip install eclipse-sumo traci sumolib rtree
  • Download and unzip the SUMO scenario; run python run_scenario.py --controller fixed
  • Optional: install sumo-gui from eclipse.dev/sumo for the visual labs
  • Pre-work (about 90 minutes): Layer 1 of the Study path and its quiz
  • Bring one transport problem from your own organisation

Facilitators and room

  • Two facilitators: a lead and a lab assistant (one per 12 participants in labs)
  • Projector, whiteboard, sticky notes, printed cheat sheets and lab manuals
  • Reliable Wi-Fi and power for every seat
  • Tables for teams of 3–5
  • Backup: USB sticks with the scenario and Python wheels
  • Test the full lab sequence on a clean laptop the day before
  • Print certificates; prepare the pre/post quiz
Assessment

How progress is measured

During the workshop

  • Pre-quiz (day 1, 09:00) and post-quiz (day 3, 16:00): the same 15 questions drawn from the three layer quizzes.
  • Lab check-offs: each lab has an expected result in the lab manual.
  • Exit tickets at the end of each day: one thing learned, one question.
  • Capstone scored with the rubric above.

Certificate of completion

  • At least 80% attendance
  • Labs 1–4 checked off (engineers) or the design worksheets completed (managers)
  • Capstone presented

Organisations may add CPD hours in line with their professional body (about 19.5 contact hours, excluding breaks).

Feedback form (end of day 3)

  1. How well did the workshop meet its stated outcomes? (1–5)
  2. Which session was most useful, and why?
  3. Which session should change, and how?
  4. Was the pace of the labs right? (too slow / right / too fast)
  5. How likely are you to start a twin pilot in the next 12 months? (1–5)
  6. What support would you need to do that?
